# Reply Playbook — Quick Reference

## The Rules
1. Reply to 15-20 tweets per day
2. Never say "great post!" or "love this!"
3. Add something specific — a number, a story, a disagreement
4. Mention CalmPilot only when it's genuinely relevant (max 1 in 5 replies)
5. Keep replies under 280 characters when possible — punchy wins

## Reply Templates by Situation

### Someone complains about email overload
> I timed it — I was spending 40 min/day just catching up on email before starting real work. The problem isn't email. It's that we've accepted checking as "work."

### Someone praises Zapier/Make/n8n
> Honest question: how many steps in your automation? I used Zapier for 2 years and loved it until I had to debug a Zap at 11pm. "No-code" shouldn't mean "different code."

### Someone talks about AI agents
> The gap isn't intelligence — it's action. Most AI tools today tell you what to do. The next wave is AI that's actually connected to your tools and can execute, not just advise.

### Someone shares morning routine
> Missing step: the 30-40 min inbox catch-up nobody counts as part of the routine. I started timing mine and realized I wasn't starting "work" until 9:45 most days.

### Someone posts build-in-public numbers
> [Comment on a specific number they shared]. I'm at [your current followers] building CalmPilot — the early days are humbling but the compounding is real.

### Someone asks "what AI tool for X?"
> Depends on whether you want advice or action. ChatGPT writes great answers. But it can't check your calendar, read your email, or do anything about what it finds. That's the gap.

### Someone talks about focus/deep work
> The biggest focus killer isn't notifications — it's the catch-up tax. That 30-40 min every morning where you're just figuring out what happened while you were asleep. Eliminate that and your whole day shifts.

### Someone launches a product
> Congrats on shipping. What's your distribution plan? I just launched and the hardest lesson was: the product being ready is 20% of the work. Getting people to see it is the other 80%.
